Planning documents set out what a development will look like and what it must
provide. In Darwin Green, the schools, parks, community rooms, retail units,
library, bus routes, and affordable homes are promised, with deadlines, in a
set of planning documents. Knowing where to find them may help you understand
what is supposed to happen, when, and who is responsible for it.

Darwin Green is at the boundary between Cambridge City and South
Cambridgeshire. Planning for both councils is handled jointly by **Greater
Cambridge Shared Planning** (GCSP). The largest Darwin Green applications are
decided by the councils' **Joint Development Management Committee** (JDMC,
formerly Joint Development Control Committee).

## Establishing a large development

A development of the size of Darwin Green goes through several layers of
documents. They are listed here, by increasing level of detail:

1. **Local Plan**: the councils' long-term, statutory plan *allocates* the
   land for housing and sets out what the development must provide. Planning
   applications are decided in accordance with the Local Plan, unless there
   are good reasons not to.
2. **Outline planning permission** (reference ending in `/OUT`): agrees the
   principle of the development, such as the maximum number of homes, the
   main uses, and the broad layout. Most details are "reserved" for later.
   The permission comes with conditions, listed in its **decision notice**:
   Darwin Green 1's has dozens of them. Its **parameter plans** and **design
   code** set rules that every later phase must follow.
3. **Section 106 agreement** (S106): a legal agreement under Section 106 of
   the Town and Country Planning Act 1990. It is signed alongside the outline
   permission by the councils, the landowners, and the developer. It secures
   what the development must deliver to make it acceptable: land for schools,
   community buildings, open space, affordable housing, and payments to the
   councils.

   The agreement is tied to the land. Many obligations come with a
   **trigger**, often a number of homes occupied ("before the 100th dwelling
   is occupied").
4. **Reserved matters** (reference ending in `/REM`): detailed applications
   for each phase or parcel, made under the outline permission. They cover the
   design, such as layout, appearance, and landscaping.
5. **Discharge of conditions** (for example `07/0003/COND70E`): the
   developer submits the details required by a condition of the permission,
   such as drainage, parking, or construction hours, for the council's
   approval.
6. **Changes along the way**: non-material amendments (`/NMA`), variations of
   conditions (`/S73`), and S106 **deeds of variation** change earlier
   permissions and agreements.

## Who does what

**Greater Cambridge Shared Planning** (GCSP) is the planning service shared by
Cambridge City Council and South Cambridgeshire District Council. Its officers:

* prepare the Local Plans;
* assess planning applications. They decide smaller applications themselves,
  and make a recommendation to the councillors of the JDMC for the largest
  ones, such as those in Darwin Green;
* approve the details submitted to discharge conditions;
* negotiate S106 agreements and monitor their delivery;
* investigate breaches of planning control, such as work that doesn't follow
  its permission. They usually try to resolve these by negotiation before
  taking formal enforcement steps.

Cambridgeshire County Council monitors the obligations it receives, such as
school payments and highway works. Appeals are decided by the Planning
Inspectorate.

## Darwin Green 1's S106

The main S106 agreement for Darwin Green 1 was signed in December 2013. It is
over 170 pages long, with 27 schedules. They cover, among other things,
education, open space, community provisions, affordable housing, highways, and
payments to the councils. A few examples:

* **Affordable housing**: 40% of the homes are to be affordable, with the mix
  of tenures set out in the agreement.
* **Schools**: the developer must transfer a serviced site for a primary school
  to the County Council. It must also prepare the transfer of a secondary
  school site by the 450th home occupied (or when the school gets permission,
  if later).
* **Community facilities**: the agreement provides for a community building, a
  library and a health centre in the local centre. For example, the community
  café was originally due before the 101st home was occupied. A deed of
  variation in 2021 renamed it "Community Rooms" and changed its timetable:
  temporary rooms before the 50th home, and permanent rooms before the 300th
  home, with no more than 500 homes occupied until they are ready.
* **Open space**: the Central Park, allotments, play areas, and other green
  spaces, with rules on how they are laid out and transferred to the City
  Council, and payments for their maintenance.
* **Transport**: payments towards bus services, bus and cycle priority, and
  cycle routes outside the site, such as on Histon Road.
* **Smaller items**: a community development worker, a "Community Chest", air
  quality monitoring, sports pitch contributions, and more.

The agreement has since been changed by several deeds of variation:

* The 2014 deed swaps the deadlines for the allotment sites in BDW3 and BDW5/6.
* The 2018 deed brings technical changes for housing associations and their
  lenders.
* The 2021 deed moves a drainage pond onto Darwin Green 2 land, and turns the
  community café into "Community Rooms".

They are all published with the outline application, `07/0003/OUT`.

Money paid under S106 agreements is reported each year in the councils'
**Infrastructure Funding Statements** (see [the related
links](#additional-sources)).

## Where to find the documents

### The planning portal

Planning applications, with all their documents, are published on the [Greater
Cambridge Shared Planning portal][portal]. To find an application:

1. Open the portal, and search by application reference (for example
   `07/0003/OUT`) or by address.
2. Open the application, and select the **Documents** tab.
3. You can sort the list by document type or date. Look for types such as
   "Application Decision Notice", "Application Committee / Delegated Report",
   or "Application Legal Agreement/S106/UU".

Applications for a phase often come with many related applications: discharges
of conditions, amendments, or deeds of variation filed under a different
reference. To find them, search the portal for the reference of the
application (for example `16/0208/REM`): the results include the applications
that mention it.

Applications can have hundreds of documents. The following ones are good places
to start:

* The **committee report** (or officer report) is the planning officer's
  write-up of the application for the committee that decides on it, with a
  recommendation. It is often the clearest summary of the application, its
  history, and the objections it received. For smaller applications, decided
  by officers, the equivalent is the delegated report.
* The **decision notice** is the formal decision (approval or refusal), with
  the conditions attached.

## Policy documents

Local Plans, which allocate the Darwin Green site:

* [Cambridge Local Plan 2018][clp2018]: Darwin Green 1 is allocated under
  Policy 20.
* [South Cambridgeshire Local Plan 2018][sclp2018]: Darwin Green 2 and 3 are
  allocated under Policy SS/2.
* The emerging [Greater Cambridge Local Plan][gclp], prepared by the two
  councils, covers the three phases of Darwin Green under Policy S/HHR. It is
  due to be submitted for examination at the end of 2026.

Guidance on S106 agreements:

* The [Greater Cambridge Planning Obligations Supplementary Planning
  Document][spd] (2026) explains how the councils decide what new S106
  agreements should contain. It does not affect agreements that are already
  signed.

## Additional sources

* **Committee papers**: agendas, reports and minutes of the Joint Development
  Management Committee, on the [Cambridge City Council democracy
  website][democracy].
* **Infrastructure Funding Statements**: published each December, they report
  S106 money received and spent. See the [Cambridge City Council][ifs-city] and
  [Cambridgeshire County Council][ifs-county] statements for 2024/25; the
  latter covers school and transport contributions. Later statements are
  published on the [City Council][ifs-city-index] and [County
  Council][ifs-county-index] websites.
* **S106 delivery**: the [planning obligations portal][pfm] shows S106 money
  due and received, and works due and delivered, for each development. You can
  ask GCSP for [written confirmation][s106-info] that S106 obligations have
  been met, or use GCSP's [online form][enforcement] to report work that
  doesn't follow its permission.
* **Planning appeals**: decisions on appeals are on the [Planning
  Inspectorate's website][pins].
* **Tracking new applications**: [PlanIt][planit] lists and maps planning
  applications, and can be easier to browse than the portal. You should always
  check the official portal for the documents themselves.

Anyone can comment on a planning application while it is being considered: see

DGRA occasionally discusses planning matters at [its
meetings][general-meetings]. It also takes part in the [community planning

present updates on Darwin Green. All of these are public, don't hesitate to
join the discussions!
