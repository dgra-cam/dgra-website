## Origin of the Names in Darwin Green

_Darwin Green_ is a reference to [Charles Darwin] (1809--1882), the famous
naturalist, geologist, and biologist. But do you know where the names of the
streets and buildings in the development come from? The builder company hasn't
published the origin of the names, but here's our guess:

Balfour Lane

: Darwin compared [Francis Maitland Balfour] (1851--1882), embryologist,
naturalist, and fellow of Trinity College in Cambridge, with [Georges Cuvier]
(1769--1832), implying respect for his expertise in comparative anatomy. He
died aged 30 while attempting the first ascent of the Aiguille Blanche, in the
Mont Blanc massif. He was the younger brother of [Arthur Balfour] (1848--1930),
Prime Minister of the United Kingdom from 1902 to 1905.

  He appears to be unrelated to [John Hutton Balfour] (1808--1884), a
distinguished Scottish botanist and Professor of Botany, who -- during his
studies at the University of Edinburgh -- joined the Plinian Society, a natural
history club for students that Charles Darwin attended at the same period.

Beagle Road

: [HMS _Beagle_][beagle] was the ship that carried Darwin around the world, for
a five-year trip that established the scientist's status as an eminent
geologist and naturalist, and played a key role in the formation of his
theories on evolution.

Cornford Drive

: [Frances Cornford] (1886--1960), née Darwin, classicist and poet, was Charles
Darwin's granddaughter. She is buried in the Parish of the Ascension Burial
Ground, like several others of Darwin's family.

Crick Road

: [Francis Crick] (1916--2004) was a molecular biologist whose joint discovery
of the helical structure of the DNA molecule, announced at the Eagle pub in
1953, earned him a Nobel Prize. His grandfather [Walter Drawbridge Crick]
(1857--1903), English businessman, amateur geologist and palaeontologist,
corresponded with Charles Darwin.

Darwin building

: A direct reference to [Charles Darwin]. Darwin had ties with Cambridge: he
studied at Christ's College from 1828 to 1831 before leaving for his famous
voyage, and once returned he spent a few months (mid-December 1836 to early
March 1837) in Cambridge to arrange expert classification of his collections
while preparing his own research for publication.

Evolution Court

: Darwin was instrumental to the conception of the theory of [evolution] by
natural selection. He developed his theory over the twenty years following his
voyage aboard HMS _Beagle_.

Falmouth Avenue

: [Falmouth] is a town on the River Fal on the south coast of Cornwall. On 2nd
October 1836, HMS _Beagle_ anchored at Falmouth, thus ending [its second
trip][beagle-trip] and bringing back Darwin to England.

Falmouth Close

: See _Falmouth Avenue_.

Galton Road

: [Francis Galton] (1822--1911), explorer, statistician, and eugenicist, was
Darwin's half-cousin. He studied mathematics at Trinity College in Cambridge,
and is credited for ideas that became the basis of [behavioural genetics].

Hawking building

: This is probably a reference to the theoretical physicist and cosmologist
[Stephen Hawking] (1942--2018), whose ashes were interred at Westminster Abbey,
between the graves of Sir Isaac Newton and Charles Darwin.

Henslow Crescent

: The Reverend [John Stevens Henslow] (1796--1861) was an Anglican priest,
botanist and geologist, interested in the variation within species of plants.
He was Darwin's friend and academic advisor, and Darwin described their
friendship as the most important circumstance in his whole career. Henslow was
the first person to be offered the position of naturalist on the voyage of HMS
_Beagle_, which he declined because of family responsibilities. He suggested
Darwin as a suitable naturalist instead.

Horace Walk

: [Sir Horace Darwin] (1851--1928) was an engineer specialising in the design
and manufacture of precision scientific instruments, and the ninth child of
Charles Darwin. He was also a Fellow of Trinity College in Cambridge, and was
elected Mayor of the city between 1896 and 1897.

Jenyns Drive

: An English clergyman, author, and naturalist, the Reverend [Leonard Jenyns]
(1800--1893) was another scholar mentored by John Stevens Henslow. Jenyns was
the second person to be offered the position of naturalist on the second voyage
of HMS _Beagle_ but turned down the offer due to ill health and parish duties.
He and Henslow suggested Charles Darwin as his replacement.

Lawrence Weaver Road

: [Sir Lawrence Weaver][lwww] (1876--1930) was an English architectural writer
and civil servant. He founded the National Institute of Agricultural Botany in
1919 under the motto "better seeds: better crops" to help safeguard food
supplies after World War I. Built in 2011, Lawrence Weaver Road predates the
Darwin Green development, which explains why it has no ties with Darwin.

Lewis building

: This is a tricky name, because there's no apparent well-known relationship
between Darwin and anyone named Lewis. Based on the names of the neighbour
buildings (Hawking, Darwin, Newton), we suppose it could be a scientist and/or
an individual buried in Westminster Abbey. A good candidate would be [C. S.
Lewis][lewis] (1898--1963), writer, literary scholar, and theologian, who
authored _The Chronicles of Narnia_. He held a chair at Magdalene College in
Cambridge from 1954 to 1963, and was honoured, 50 years after his death, with a
memorial in Poets' Corner in Westminster Abbey.

Newton building

: [Sir Isaac Newton] (1642--1727) was a scientist whose legacy is too long to
list in this paragraph but includes the laws of motion and universal
gravitation. He was a fellow of Trinity College in Cambridge, and served twice
as Member of Parliament for the University of Cambridge. After his death he was
buried in Westminster Abbey, where Darwin later joined him.

Patagonia Way

: The southern end of South America, [Patagonia] is a region spreading over
Argentina and Chile. About his voyage aboard HMS _Beagle_, [Darwin
wrote][patagonia-journal]: "The object of the expedition was to complete the
survey of Patagonia and Tierra del Fuego, commenced under Captain King in 1826
to 1830 -- to survey the shores of Chile, Peru, and of some islands in the
Pacific -- and to carry a chain of chronometrical measurements round the
World."

Plymouth Close

: On his second voyage, the one with Darwin on board, HMS _Beagle_ departed
Devonport, Plymouth Sound, near [Plymouth], on 27 December 1831. Plymouth was
also the birth town of [William Elford Leach] (1791--1836), a zoologist and
marine biologist who paved the way for Darwin's work.

Randal Way

: This may relate to [Randal Keynes] (1948--2023), conservationist, author, and
great-great-grandson of Charles Darwin. Randal was born in Cambridge, and
authored _Annie's Box_, a book about the relationship between Darwin and his
daughter Annie, who died at the age of 10, probably of tuberculosis.

Sedgwick Crescent

: The Reverend [Adam Sedgwick] (1785--1873) was a British geologist and
Anglican priest, a fellow of Trinity College in Cambridge, and one of the
founders of modern geology. He guided Charles Darwin in his study of geology
and its practical methods on a field trip to North Wales, before the voyage of
the _Beagle_. A conservative cleric, Sedgwick was never reconciled to Darwin's
ideas on the origin of species. In spite of this, the two scholars remained on
good terms. The Sedgwick Museum in Cambridge also bears his name.

Shrewsbury Road

: [Shrewsbury] is a town in Shropshire, where Charles Darwin was born and went
to school, and where his father was a prosperous doctor.

Wallace Way

: [Alfred Russel Wallace] (1823--1913) was a naturalist, explorer, geographer,
anthropologist, biologist, and illustrator. He was a friend to Darwin, and he
independently conceived the theory of evolution through natural selection. A
joint paper on the subject was presented at a meeting of the Linnean Society in
1858, but drew little reaction. This pushed Darwin to publish his book _On the
Origin of Species_: the first edition in 1859 became a best-seller and went
into six editions in Darwin's lifetime.

Wedgewood Avenue

: With a close spelling: [Josiah Wedgwood II] (1769--1843), son of [Josiah
Wedgwood] (1730--1795), was Charles Darwin's uncle and convinced [Robert
Darwin] (1766--1848), Charles' father (and Josiah's brother-in-law), to approve
and fund Charles' place on HMS _Beagle_. The [Darwin and Wedgwood families]
were closely allied. Charles' mother, [Susannah Darwin] (1765--1817, née
Wedgwood), was sister to Josiah Wedgwood II, and Charles married [Emma Darwin]
(1808--1896, née Wedgwood), Josiah's daughter.

  We don't know about "Wedgewood" (with an "e"). Could it be a mistake?

Wren House

: [Wrens] belong to a family of small brown passerine birds. During his voyage,
Darwin collected a specimen that he described as a Galápagos "wren", but turned
out to be another closely allied species of finch.

## Origin of the Names in Franklin Gardens

Street names in Franklin Gardens mostly have a connection with Darwin, or with
biology. As for the name of the development, it is unclear who Franklin refers
to. We like to believe the name comes from [Rosalind Franklin] (1920--1958), a
chemist whose work was central to the understanding of the molecular structures
of DNA, RNA, viruses, and more. She was largely unrecognised during her life,
but has earned posthumous recognition since the end of the 20th century. Or
maybe the name relates to [Benjamin Franklin] (1706--1790), writer, scientist,
inventor, statesman, diplomat, printer, publisher, political philosopher, and
one of the founding fathers of the United States of America, who had
established a lifelong friendship with [Erasmus Darwin] (1731--1802), Charles'
grandfather.

Now, here's our guess for the streets:

Bearsden Avenue

: [Bearsden] is a town north-west of Glasgow, in Scotland. We couldn't find any
ties with Darwin.

Covington Way

: [Syms Covington] (1816--1861) was a fiddler and cabin boy on HMS _Beagle_. He
became an assistant to Charles Darwin and was appointed as his full-time
personal servant in 1833, for £30 a year. He assisted Darwin in preserving
specimens during the voyage on the _Beagle_.

Down House Drive

: [Down House] is the former home of Charles Darwin, south of Downe, a village
south-east of London. This was Darwin's retreat from the busyness of London,
and the place where he died in 1882. Down House, its garden and grounds are now
open to the public.

Galapagos Road

: The [Galápagos Islands] are an archipelago of volcanic islands in the Eastern
Pacific, located around the equator, part of the Republic of Ecuador. During
its second voyage, HMS _Beagle_ took Darwin to the archipelago, where the
scientist studied the geology of the islands as well as many animal species.
This eventually led to his proposal of the theory of evolution. One of the
islands is named after Darwin.

Genome Close

: A [genome] is the complete set of genetic instructions required to build an
organism.

Iguana Way

: The [Iguanidae] is a family of lizards, including, for example, the
[Galápagos land iguana], some of the largest lizards in the world. During his
voyage, Darwin studied these iguanas and described them as "ugly animals". He
also reported that someone called [marine iguanas] "imps of darkness".

Malthus Close

: [Thomas Robert Malthus] (1766--1834) was an English economist, cleric, and
scholar influential in the fields of political economy and demography. In _An
Essay on the Principle of Population_, he theorised that the population would
double every 25 years, but food production would fail to follow, leading to
famine unless birth rates decreased. His work played a role in the development
of Darwin and Wallace's ideas: Darwin observed that population growth would
lead to a "struggle for existence" where "favourable variations" prevailed.

Nature Road

: That's, you know, [nature]. Darwin's theory of evolution contributed to
changing the understanding we have of nature.

Tahiti Road

: [Tahiti] is the largest and most populated island in French Polynesia, a set
of volcanic archipelagos in the central part of the Pacific Ocean. Darwin
visited the island during his voyage on HMS _Beagle_.

Wallace Road

: See _Wallace Way_ in Darwin Green.
