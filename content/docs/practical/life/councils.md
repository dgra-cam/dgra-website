## Councils & Authorities

The **Cambridge City Council** and the **Cambridgesire County Council** are two
distinct entities that both exerce authority in Cambridge, each with its own
responsibilities.

### Cambridge City Council

* Website: <https://www.cambridge.gov.uk>
* Phone: 01223 457000
* Email: <enquiries@cambridge.gov.uk>
* Address: Cambridge City Council PO Box 700, Cambridge CB1 OJH
* [Facebook][fb-camcitco], [X/Twitter][x-camcitco]

[fb-camcitco]: https://www.facebook.com/camcitco/
[x-camcitco]: https://x.com/camcitco

Contact Cambridge City Council for information on:

* Sport and leisure
* Community development
* Voting and local councillors
* Council tax
* Bins, rubbish and recycling
* Benefits and support
* Planning
* Homelessness and housing advice

When moving in, don't forget to register for Council Tax. The occupant is
responsible for paying council tax. Tell the Council as soon as you have moved
in to ensure that you are charged the correct amount.

Cambridgeshire County Council covers the city of Cambridge, but also the
surrouding district councils: East Cambridgeshire, Fenland, Huntingdonshire,
and South Cambridgeshire District Councils.

{{% alert color="info" title="Local Government Reorganisation" %}}
By April 2028, the Cambridgeshire County Council is expected to disappear, and
the district councils are supposed to be grouped into Unitary Authorities,
leaving only one entity in charge of a given area instead of the current
two-tier (Cambridge City and Cambridgeshire County) system.

Cambridge City Council is expected to merge with South Cambridgeshire District
Council, and maybe East Cambridgeshire District Council, depending on the
decision from the government, to form a Unitary Authority that will also be
responsible for what Cambridgeshire County Council provides today.

For more information, see [Cambridge City Council's FAQ on Local Government
Reorganisation][faq-lgr].

[faq-lgr]: https://www.cambridge.gov.uk/local-government-reorganisation-faqs
{{% /alert %}}

### Cambridgeshire and Peterborough Combined Authority

Created in 2017, [Cambridgeshire and Peterborough Combined Authority][cpca] has
a range of responsibilities including transport, infrastructure, planning,
economic growth, business support and adult education.

[cpca]: https://cambridgeshirepeterborough-ca.gov.uk/

## Other relevant government bodies

### Greater Cambridge Shared Planning

[Greater Cambridge Shared Planning][gcsp] (GCSP) is the shared planning service
between Cambridge City Council and South Cambridgeshire District Council. It is
involved in the planning of developments such as Darwin Green.

[gcsp]: https://greatercambridgeplanning.org/

### South Cambridgeshire District Council

Darwin Green lies on the border of Cambridge City and South Cambridgeshire
District Council and you may wish to find out more about the services that fall
within their remit.

For information visit the [South Cambridgeshire District Council's
website][scambs].

[scambs]: https://www.scambs.gov.uk

## Local democracy and Councillors

To find out who your local Cambridge City Councillors are, visit the [Cambridge
City Council's website][camcitco] and search for "Castle" ward.

To find out who your local Cambridgeshire County Councillors are, visit [the
Councillors webpage from the County Council's website][cambscc-councillors].

[cambscc-councillors]: https://www.cambridgeshire.gov.uk/council/county-councillors

### Voting in Castle ward

Castle ward is the electoral ward covering Darwin Green. When moving in, do not
forget to register to vote or to update your details, so you can vote at the
next elections, if you have the right to do so. For more details on how to
register for voting, look at the [City Council's page on voting][voting].

Local elections include voting for:

- [Cambridge City Council members][city-elec]
- [Cambridgeshire County Council members][county-elec]
- [The Mayor of Cambridgeshire and Peterborough][cpca-mayor]
- [The Police and Crime Commissioner][police-com]

Each ward has three seats at the Cambridge City Council. One seat in each ward
is up for election each year. This happens for three years in a row, then
elections to the county council are held every fourth year. The Mayor of
Cambridgeshire and Peterborough and the Police and Crime Commissioner also run
on 4-year mandates.
