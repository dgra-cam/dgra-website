## Logo

Here is the logo for the association:

![DGRA logo: stylised, monochrome hummingbird and heron in a circle](/dgra-green.svg)

The hummingbird and the heron are references to Charles Darwin and Cambridge,
respectively.

During his trips, Darwin studied a number of birds, and his observations
eventually led to the conception of the theory of evolution. As it turns out,
most of the birds that Darwin studied were not hummingbirds, but finches or
mockingbirds. Nevertheless, in the collective imagination hummingbirds remain
strongly associated to the scientist; the legacy £10 bank notes may have played
a role in this. See also [_Darwin's Hummingbird_][hummingbird-paper] from Iris
Montero Sobrevilla. A hummingbird also has the advantage of being easier to
represent in a distinctive fashion on a logo.

[hummingbird-paper]: https://www.jstor.org/stable/j.ctv1vbd275.44

Although not a formal emblem, the heron is a bird that can frequently be
observed in Cambridge, both as an live animal around the river Cam, and as
[murals].

[murals]: https://www.cambridge-news.co.uk/news/cambridge-news/herons-plaques-around-cambridge-where-16162669
