The [North West and West Cambridge Community
Forum](https://cambridge.gov.uk/community-forums) held a meeting on 11th
June 2026, with Barratt, the County Council library service, and different
stakeholders reporting on the progress on the development. Here are some
notes from the meeting.

For previous updates, see [the report from the previous Community
Forum](../2026-02-26-community-forum-report/).

_Please keep in mind that these are the notes from attendees. They may be
incomplete, inaccurate, and were not validated by Barratt or Eddington
representatives._

## General Planning update

[Previous notes](../2026-02-26-community-forum-report/#general-planning-update)

This section was presented by Philippa Kelly, Projects Delivery Manager at GCSP, who also chaired the meeting.

The **Greater Cambridge Local Plan** is progressing. A proposed submission
public consultation is expected in summer 2026; submission to the Secretary of
State should happen in December 2026.

The Government is establishing **a [Development Corporation][devcorp] for
Greater Cambridge** as "a regeneration body to accelerate growth", to unblock
and take care of strategic developments. South Cambridgeshire District Council
has warned that the proposed urban development corporation risks sidelining
local communities. For more details, see [_Greater Cambridge gets regeneration
body to accelerate growth_ on gov.uk][devcorp-pr] or [the FAQ from the
Cambridge Growth Company][cgc] (CGC). CGC also has [a mailing list][cgc-ml],
for those willing to get updates about the new Development Corporation.

## Library update

For the first time, we had a presentation from Andrew Stoter, from the Library
Support Services at Cambridgeshire County Council! The core offer will include:

- Book stock of 5,000 to 6,000 books
- Free Wi-Fi
- Study space
- Static PCs, printing and scanning
- Staffed hours and extended unstaffed opening via the [Open+] scheme (access
  beyond staffed hours, on evenings or weekends -- approximately 60 hours per
  week in total; restricted to adults aged 16+ who register and sign in)
- Interview rooms for engagement with the Council and other services
  (councillors, social workers, NHS, etc.)

The public art piece on the windows of the building is [_Darwin's Tree_, by
Tricia Mackinnon-Day][dtree].

Estimated timeline for the library:

- June 2026: public engagement survey (closes **11th July 2026**)
- August 2026: lease signed
- September 2026: procurement
- **April 2027: opening at latest, but likely earlier (Barratt hopes for
  November/December 2026)**

**Please consider [responding to the survey][lib-survey]**. It covers opening
hours, space allocation, book stock priorities, activities, and volunteering.
You do not have to be a Darwin Green resident to complete it, but it's better
if you intend to use the library. State what you would like to use, rather than
what you think the library should have.

[Open+]: https://www.cambridgeshire.gov.uk/residents/libraries-leisure-culture/libraries/visit-a-library/open-plus-out-of-hours-library-access

[dtree]: https://mackinnon-day.com/projects/darwin-green/

### Q&A

- Meeting rooms redundancy: The library's interview rooms will be smaller 1:1
  spaces rather than the larger meeting room available in the Community Rooms.

- Health services redundancy: The library's health-related provision is
  envisaged as light-touch support (for example, midwife drop-ins or additional
  services) rather than a facility that would compete with the NHS Health
  Centre.

- Exhibiting artwork: The library is planning a hanging system to allow
  artworks to be displayed.

- Maintenance costs: The City Council will be paying service charges for the
  library building; leaseholders will not be expected to contribute to
  long-term maintenance costs.

## Darwin Green 1

[Previous notes](../2026-02-26-community-forum-report/#darwin-green-1)

This section was presented by Andrew MacLaren, Project Manager for Darwin Green
1 at Barratt Redrow.

### Site-wide overview

As of June 2026, there are **736 dwellings occupied** in Darwin Green 1: 397
are private (for sale) and 339 are affordable dwellings.

Development progress:

- Parcel BDW2: approximately 75% complete, a few months away from completion
- Parcel BDW4: approximately 65% complete, around a year away from completion
- Parcel BDW3: site start expected within the next month (June/July 2026)

Parcel BDW3 will comprise 210 units and will feature a new development
"gateway" via Windsor Road, with a slightly different architectural approach
to earlier parcels. Construction is expected to take two to three years. The
parcel will also include the second of three allotment areas.

### BDW2 south-eastern ditch

Following meetings with residents, Barratt says they have listened to
concerns about the ditch. The amount of water running through it has been
significantly reduced (by about 90%) compared to pre-development levels, and it
will be populated with ecologically sensitive flora. Hedgehogs will be able to
move through dedicated holes in the fence, and to hide in the vegetation (foxes
are present). Residents expressed additional concerns about the height of the
ditch, Barratt is aware and confirms that the ditch will achieve what it is
supposed to.

### Pebbles on Plaza

(Intersection between Galton Road and Patagonia Way)

At least 8 incidents involving cars and pebbles have been reported. Barratt
confirmed that the approved works are in accordance with planning permission.
Lighting units are being checked and discussions with the City Council are
ongoing. Barratt hopes that better lighting will help. The intersection is
confusing by design, to make drivers pause. Priority: pedestrians, then
cyclists, then cars.

## Darwin Green 2 & 3

[Previous notes](../2026-02-26-community-forum-report/#darwin-green-2--3)

This section was presented by Will Hodgson, Development Director at David
Wilson Homes.

### Work streams

Work Stream 1: Design Code (in progress)

: Following the community drop-in session and Testing Day in March 2026, minor
adjustments have been made. The Design Code is now with the landowner
consortium for approval ahead of formal submission. Target submission to the
Council by August 2026.

Work Stream 2: Pre-Reserved Matters Planning Conditions (complete)

: Two key site-wide conditions have been discharged:

  - Condition 8 (Site-Wide Phasing Strategy) was approved in March 2026
  - Condition 10 (Site-Wide Youth & Play Strategy) was approved in January 2026

Work Stream 3: Infrastructure Reserved Matters (in progress)

: First Reserved Matters Application pursuant to the outline permission,
covering all key infrastructure: the primary road (including footways and
cycleways), drainage, landscaping, areas of play, and utilities. Updates:

  - Target submission to the Council: September 2026
  - Approval currently targeted: December 2026 (subject to Design Code approval)

Work Stream 4: Country Park Reserved Matters (in progress)

: 48 hectares have been set aside for the Country Park. Progress:

  - Pre-application engagement commenced in January 2026
  - Target submission to the Council: September/October 2026
  - Approval currently targeted: December 2026/January 2027

### Schools

An open meeting will take place in the next months to discuss timescales and
design aspirations for the schools; Barratt is working closely with the County
Council. Community drop-in sessions are also planned in the coming months.

### Q&A

- **Water supply**: The approval was granted by the Secretary of State
  following a public inquiry and was not considered a sufficient reason to hold
  back development. The water credit scheme is still planned; a planning
  condition requires 95 litres per household per day.

- **Active travel measures** will be included in the various planning
  applications as they come forward.

- Traffic and Country Park **parking**: Details are yet to be confirmed;
  visitor parking is anticipated but has not been finalised.

- **Bus services**: Darwin Green 2 & 3 is designed to accommodate future bus
  services; the through-route via Franklin Gardens must be complete before this
  can happen.

- **Farm** at Impington on edge of Country Park: It would be good to involve
  the community? Barratt replied the first community engagement session is
  planned for next month.

- Buildings and **increasing heat**: All new houses are to be built with the
  new standards.

## Eddington & West Cambridge

[Previous notes](../2026-02-26-community-forum-report/#eddington--west-cambridge)

This section was presented by Matt Johnson, Head of Development at Cambridge
University.

### Future Phases: planning application update

Application timeline for the Outline Planning Application:

- April 2026: amendment materials submitted
- June 2026: Council administration confirmed; new Joint Development Management
  Committee (JDMC) confirmed, with first meeting on 17th June
- Summer 2026: determination expected

### Current work on Phase 1

- **NHS Health Centre**: Lease signed; fit-out works under way. Opening
  expected in 2027 (Huntingdon practice is likely to take an office there).
- LUMO has launched, opening a new part of Eddington.
- A planning application has been submitted for the **Eddington Avenue
  Recreation Grounds and Eddington Community Gardens**, featuring football
  pitches (to be lit), cricket pitches, tennis courts, and a first phase of
  community growing space (like allotments, but less permanent because a large
  portion of residents are transient in Eddington).
- **Summer roadworks** on Eddington Avenue are planned to improve conditions
  for pedestrians.

### Placemaking

- Eddington Beer Garden: 3,000 people
- Ecology tours
- Sports in the Square: Next one on 13 June
- Park Yoga
- **Eddington Carnival**: 17 and 18 July 2026. **Local groups and talent are
  invited to get in touch to participate**.

### Q&A

- **Build-to-rent** and short-term lets: Residents raised concerns about
  build-to-rent homes being converted into Airbnb-style accommodation. The
  University confirmed this is not its intention; it is working with residents
  to understand concerns and will seek to maintain control over the use in
  future phases.

## General Q&A

- **Bus services around the Plaza**: Residents asked what can be done to route
  the U1, 5, and 6 bus services past the Plaza. The T2 bus service runs along
  Histon and Huntingdon Roads. Barratt confirmed that a through-route to the
  other end of Franklin Gardens must first be complete before a bus route can
  pass through. Barratt will look into the obligation and see whether a
  temporary pass route around the Plaza is doable. Philippa Kelly from Greater
  Cambridge Shared Planning undertook to follow this up and will reply in
  writing. Someone mentioned the Combined Authority may also have something to
  say into the matter.

- **Where to park** for the library and other services? Access by sustainable
  travel modes is being promoted as the priority. Visitor parking spots exist
  near the Sports Pavilion, but the development is otherwise car-free, with a
  £100 fine for unauthorised parking.

- **Girton College event**: Everyone is invited to the event organised by
  Girton College undergraduates, on Sunday 21 June!
